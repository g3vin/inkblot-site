# Dress Inkblot like a film house, not SaaS

Inkblot's site should borrow the restraint of film production companies, photobook publishers and literary photo portfolios, then add one gesture that is clearly ink. That means a warm paper ground, one serif, typewritten metadata, a single Ink Blue accent and a real product loop in the hero. The fit is already there. Inkblot's own "Quiet Studio" tokens (paper `#faf8f4`, ink `#1f1c18`, Ink Blue `#2f4a8a`, Literata + Inter + Courier Prime) are close to what admired film and photo houses use: Hungryman's `#f7f4ed`/`#232323`, Harbor's paper-and-navy, Pulse Films' mono-on-paper, Metrograph's cream and navy, Siena Film Foundation's `#FAF7EF`/`#000`, and MUBI's grotesk + Tiempos + single navy. The site does not need a new look invented for it. The research also sets a hard limit on how "avant-garde" the page can be. Visitors judge visual appeal in about 50 ms and prefer simple, familiar layouts. Pages that don't explain themselves within about 10 seconds get abandoned. NN/g found 57% of viewing time above the fold and 74% within two screens. So the experiment should live in type, colour, texture and one motion moment, while the structure stays a conventional landing page: headline, real UI, "Download for macOS" with a Courier Prime spec line, proof, a repeated CTA, then a short Shipped/Now/Next timeline, then an FAQ. The things most likely to drive people away are measurable. They are slow heroes, scroll-jacking, preloaders, low-contrast "elegant" grey text (found on 83.9% of top home pages in 2026), autoplay with no pause control, and missing basics like price, platform and signing status. Several of those basics are still undecided owner choices: pricing, notarization, minimum macOS version and where public release files will be hosted. Below are four reviewable galleries, about 125 sites in all, each with a "Like? ☐" box. The film, photo and product sites were checked live; most award sites were checked through their Awwwards listings. They are followed by the evidence on placement, scroll and repellents, and then the flags for unverified statistics, dead domains and gaps.

## Inkblot's own brand already matches the film and photo dialect

The brand rules come from the app repo, and the galleries should be judged against them. Inkblot is a local-first macOS writing studio for long-form work: novels, screenplays and worldbuilding, through to typesetting and KDP/IngramSpark-ready publishing. Its design principle is **"As easy as a notes app, as deep as a publishing tool"** ([DESIGN.md](file:///Users/gavin/programming-projects/inkblot/docs/DESIGN.md)). The personality is **"calm, literate, precise and warm… not whimsical, nostalgic or themed. We never use paper textures, typewriter sound effects or coffee stains,"** and the brand shows "in small, crafted details: a typewritten label, an ink-blue dot, a well-set page. It is never loud." The tokens are warm paper neutrals (`#faf8f4`, `#f3f0ea`) and sepia-black ink (`#1f1c18`), with "Don't use #000, #fff or cool greys" for interface colour. There are eight fountain-pen inks, with **Ink Blue `#2f4a8a`** as the interface colour. The three typefaces are **Inter** for UI, **Literata** for the writer's words and **Courier Prime** only for metadata (word counts, dates, shortcuts) and the wordmark. Icons are Phosphor Light. The logo is a lowercase "ib" monogram in Literata ExtraBold whose i-dot is a **procedurally modelled ink splash**, generated by a physics script (`splash.py`) rather than drawn by hand. It is always black on white and "never takes Ink Blue" ([tokens.css](file:///Users/gavin/programming-projects/inkblot/src/styles/tokens.css); [DESIGN.md §2–5](file:///Users/gavin/programming-projects/inkblot/docs/DESIGN.md)).

Those rules screen out parts of the galleries. Grain overlays (Riff Raff, BUCK), Craft's torn-paper collage, Allume's hand-drawn annotations and the playful multi-font sites (Blinkink, Kinopio, Planetoño) break "no paper textures" and "not whimsical". Like them for energy if you wish, but they can't be copied directly. A second tension is easy to miss. Several research threads suggest a **Rorschach-symmetric blot** hero. Inkblot's actual mark, though, is an asymmetric splash "with a few droplets flung up and to the right." A hero generated from the same `splash.py` physics would be more on-brand than a mirrored Rorschach fold, and it would belong only to Inkblot. A third: Fraunces, with its "inkiness" SOFT axis ([Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Fraunces/about)), is tempting, but adding it would break the three-typeface system. Stay with Literata.

The product facts also set what the site can promise. The app is **v0.1.0 (tagged 24 Sep 2026)**, a Tauri 2 app that is **macOS-only in practice**. iPad/iPhone is planned, but on-device models are "the open question" ([ARCHITECTURE.md](file:///Users/gavin/programming-projects/inkblot/ARCHITECTURE.md)). No pricing, signing/notarization config or minimum macOS version exists yet. The app repo is private, so release files will need a public home. The privacy story is strong and documented: no accounts, no cloud sync, no analytics, no ads, ordinary files on disk, and on-device dictation, Read Aloud and synonym models ([PRIVACY.md](file:///Users/gavin/programming-projects/inkblot/PRIVACY.md)). The previous site (Playpen Sans, green accents, "The visual writing studio for creatives") conflicts with this brand and was rightly stripped. There are **no screenshots or marketing videos** in any repo yet, and the `inkblot-splatters` render folder is empty. That is the largest production gap for every direction below.

## How to read the galleries

Each gallery is a table: **Like? ☐ · Site (clickable) · Styling · What transfers to Inkblot**. Replace ☐ with ☑ as you go. "⚠" marks a conflict with Inkblot's brand rules. All sites were checked on **14 Oct 2026**, but by different methods, so confidence varies:

| Gallery | How it was checked | Confidence in the visual description |
|---|---|---|
| Film / production / post | Live HTML + CSS read with curl (fonts, hex values, video attributes, "Site by" credits); **not rendered** | Fonts and colours are high; layout and motion are inferred from class names |
| Photography / photobook / photo products | Live HTML + CSS read with curl or WebFetch; not rendered | Same as film |
| Product / app sites | Live fetch + **headless Chromium screenshots at 1440px**, viewed directly | High for static look; motion only inferred |
| Experimental / award winners | Awwwards listing metadata (palette, tags, scores); only two sites fetched live | Award data high; entries marked **[PK]** are from prior knowledge and were not re-checked |

No site's award badges could be confirmed beyond Awwwards' own pages. "Awards" are left blank for the film and photo galleries instead of guessed ([Awwwards listing](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/%23F4F4EF/?page=2)).

## Gallery 1 · Film directors, production companies, post houses and distributors

One firm, **Funkhaus (LA)**, built at least nine of them: Prettybird, Smuggler, MJZ, Caviar, Biscuit, Final Cut, Whitehouse Post, Exile and Harbor. That recipe is the "feels like a film company" signal, and it is also widely copied. Literata is what would keep Inkblot from looking like one more Funkhaus site.

## Visitors decide in 50 milliseconds, then again at 10 seconds

Two clocks run back to back. The first is visual. Lindgaard et al. (2006) flashed home pages for 50 ms and found the ratings matched those given after longer viewing ([summary](https://websiteoptimization.com/speed/tweak/blink/)). Tuch et al. (2012, Basel + Google) showed that **visual complexity is processed even at 17 ms and prototypicality soon after, and that low complexity plus a familiar layout scores highest**. High complexity hurt even when the layout was familiar ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/)). Lindgaard measured visual appeal only. Claims that the 50 ms impression also sets trust or usability are media interpretation. The second clock is comprehension. NN/g's reading of a dwell-time model says the first 10 seconds decide stay-or-leave, so a page must "clearly communicate your value proposition within 10 seconds" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). Treat that as a rule of thumb, not a controlled experiment.

That settles the "avant-garde but professional" tension. **Spend the experiment on treatment (typeface, colour, a splash, slow fades) and keep the skeleton prototypical**: logo top-left, short nav, headline, subhead, download button, product visual. The film sites in Gallery 1 already work this way. MUBI, Metrograph and A24 are typographically distinctive and structurally plain. Polish also pays a dividend. The aesthetic-usability effect (Kurosu & Kashimura, 1995) found that attractive interfaces were *perceived* as easier to use ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/aesthetic-usability-effect/)). In Fogg's Stanford study of 2,684 people, "design look" was the most-cited credibility factor at 46.1% ([Design for Hackers](https://designforhackers.com/?p=413)). Experts in the companion study weighted authorship and accuracy more ([InternetNews](https://internetnews.com/marketing/study-for-site-cred-looks-matter)). Elegant styling buys trust, but only alongside a real person, a real product and no small errors.

What the hero shows matters as much as how it looks. NN/g eye-tracking found that **decorative "feel-good" images are ignored, while product images are scrutinised** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/)). Video helps when it explains the product: Omnisend's explainer video raised sign-ups 21%, while at Fathom a static image beat a testimonial video ([Optibase](https://optibase.io/ab-testing-ideas/video-vs-image); [VWO](https://vwo.com/blog/image-outperforms-video-increases-monthly-revenue/)). So Inkblot's splash art should **frame a real UI loop, not replace it**. A NEON-style 2.39:1 letterboxed reel of an actual workflow, with the splash as the opening frame, fits both the evidence and the film taste. Video and screenshot assets don't exist yet, so this is the first production task whichever direction is chosen.

## Put the download above the fold, and the timeline after the proof

Attention falls off steeply with depth. In NN/g's 2018 eye-tracking study (120 participants, 1920×1080), **57% of viewing time was above the fold, 74% within the first two screens and 81% within three**. Users "rarely go beyond the third screenful" and scroll "when they have a reason to" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). Engagement is also falling. Contentsquare's average scroll rate was about 50% in 2022 ([Contentsquare](https://contentsquare.com/fr-fr/blog/temps-moyen-passe-page-web/)), with consumption down 6.5% in its 2025 report ([Contentsquare](https://contentsquare.com/press/2025-digital-experience-benchmarks/)). Expect half of visitors to see less than half the page. Length still pays when the product needs explaining. The longer page Conversion Rate Experts built for Moz, with testimonials and CTAs spread through it, beat a short sampler by **52%** ([Conversion Rate Experts](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/moz-case-study/)). Inkblot covers writing, worldbuilding and publishing, so it needs explanation. The answer is a front-loaded long page with the CTA repeated, not a short one.

Nobody has published a controlled study of section order, so this order follows the attention data:

| # | Section | Screen | Inkblot specifics | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | 1 | Literal headline (first two words carry meaning); one-line subhead; **"Download for macOS"** in Ink Blue; Courier Prime spec line beneath; real UI loop framed by a splash; ≤85–90% viewport tall so section 2 peeks in | 10-s rule; 57% above fold; false floors ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/illusion-of-completeness/)) |
| 2 | See it work | 2 | Before/after: plain draft → typeset book page (Halide's comparison); 2–3 short workflow loops | Product images get scrutinised; ~17% of attention on screen 2 |
| 3 | Feature index | 2–3 | Academy/Jack Davison-style typographic list: Write · Plan & worldbuild · On-device language tools · Publish, each row revealing a native-ratio loop | Layer-cake scanning ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)) |
| 4 | Proof and trust | 3 | Privacy strip ("No account. No cloud. No tracking. Your files, on your disk."); open formats (JSON Canvas, EPUB, Fountain, FDX, Word); named author | NN/g trust factors ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)) |
| 5 | Mid-page CTA | ~3 | Repeat the download block | Moz +52%; ~50% scroll rate |
| 6 | Timeline | 4 | **Shipped first**, then Now / Next / Exploring; "Last updated" stamp; link to the full changelog page | Secondary content lower; momentum value ([Fider](https://www.fider.io/blog/public-roadmaps-when-they-help-and-when-they-hurt)) |
| 7 | Final download | 4–5 | All facts: version, date, macOS minimum, architecture, size, SHA-256, release notes | Closing CTA for committed readers |
| 8 | FAQ + footer | last | Accordion: platforms, iPad/iPhone, sync, export, price, data ownership, signing | Spotted scanning |

**The download block.** The best pages use one OS-labelled primary button with a quiet line of facts. Raycast puts "macOS 14+ and Apple Silicon required · Install via Homebrew" in mono under its pill ([raycast.com](https://raycast.com)). Ghostty says "A universal binary that works on both Apple Silicon and Intel" and "Requires macOS 13+" ([Ghostty](https://ghostty.org/download)). Zed shows the version and date with a "View changelog →" link ([Zed](https://zed.dev/download)). For Inkblot, that becomes a **universal DMG**, so the Apple Silicon/Intel question never comes up (Apple Silicon can't be reliably detected from the user-agent anyway). Set the spec line in Courier Prime, Tillmans-style: *Version 0.x · 14 Oct 2026 · macOS [min] · Apple silicon & Intel · [size] · [Free / price]*. Put "Release notes →" beside it and a "sticky" download button in the header. Don't make the GitHub Releases page the main CTA. One project reported that sending users there, "where DMG is hidden by default", hurt its download flow ([CodexMonitor #418](https://upd.dev/Dimillian/CodexMonitor/issues/418)). The site is static on GitHub Pages, so a scheduled GitHub Action can write a `downloads.json` (version, date, URL, size) at build time. That avoids GitHub's ~60 requests/hour unauthenticated API limit ([modern-github-release](https://github.com/milisp/modern-github-release)). As a no-JS fallback, use `releases/latest/download/<stable-name>` links, which need version-free asset filenames. Don't reuse Tauri's `latest.json` updater URLs for the button, because they point at `.app.tar.gz` update bundles, not DMGs ([Tauri updater](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/updater/)). On phones, swap the dead button for "Inkblot is a Mac app — email me the link / copy link".

**The timeline.** No independent study shows that public roadmaps raise trust or conversion. The widely quoted Buffer figures are vendor marketing, and the documented failures (GitHub deleting ~40 stale roadmap items, Trello walking its roadmap back) show the real risk is broken promises ([Fider](https://www.fider.io/blog/public-roadmaps-when-they-help-and-when-they-hurt)). So lead with momentum. Obsidian's roadmap has short Active and Planned lists above a month-by-month "Launched" archive, with every entry linked to its changelog ([Obsidian roadmap](https://obsidian.md/roadmap/)). Now/Next/Later uses "three horizons rather than fixed delivery dates" ([ProdPad](https://prodpad.com/blog/invented-now-next-later-roadmap/)). Publish only items you are at least 80% confident of, because "users remember dates and forget caveats" ([Fider](https://www.fider.io/blog/public-roadmaps-when-they-help-and-when-they-hurt)). Inkblot's git history already supplies a credible Shipped column: development began 5 Feb 2026; focus mode came in May; Read Aloud in August; print layout and v0.1.0 on 23–24 Sep; publishing tools, screenplays and editions on 25–26 Sep; the story timeline merged by 8 Oct; the "ib" brand on 8 Oct ([CHANGELOG.md](file:///Users/gavin/programming-projects/inkblot/CHANGELOG.md)). For "Now / Next", ARCHITECTURE.md names timeline continuity checks, relative dates, chronology version history and timeline export, plus reminders while Inkblot is closed. The 3D/planetary maps sit on an unmerged branch, so label them **preview**. **iPad and iPhone belong under "Exploring", not "Next"**, because the on-device models have no iOS path yet ([ARCHITECTURE.md](file:///Users/gavin/programming-projects/inkblot/ARCHITECTURE.md)). For styling, combine three gallery finds:

- **Trim Editing's credit blocks** for each entry: tracked small-caps labels and Courier Prime values.
- **Jack Davison's Index / Thumbs toggle**, to switch between a dense list and a contact sheet of screenshots.
- **A single hairline spine that fills with ink as you scroll**, with status glyphs: a filled blot for shipped, a half blot for in progress, an outline for next and a dotted outline for exploring. Always pair each glyph with a text label.

Use semantic `<ol>` and `<time datetime>` markup, with a "Notify me" email capture in A24's "Not too often — just enough" tone.

**Copy.** Visitors read about 20% of the words on a page, and can read half of it only when it has about 111 words or fewer ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/)). That data is from 2005, so treat it as directional. In Unbounce's 2024 benchmark, copy at a 5th–7th grade reading level converted best ([DMN summary](https://dmn.ca/unbounces-2024-conversion-benchmark-report-proves-that-attention-spans-are-declining-and-so-are-conversion-rates/)). Aim for roughly 300–600 visible words on the homepage, with headings that carry the whole pitch on their own. That matches the brand's own voice rule: "plain and warm: sentence case, verb first, no jargon on the surface."

## Scroll comes from information scent; speed and hijacked scrolling make people leave

People scroll when what they can see promises more and the layout visibly continues. The main thing that stops them is a **false floor**. NN/g found that full-screen heroes, full-width rules, big gaps and weakly signalled carousels make pages look finished; on Maple.com, **6 of 8 users didn't realise the page scrolled** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/illusion-of-completeness/)). The fixes are simple: let the next section's top edge show above the fold, write specific headings that hint at the payoff, and never put crucial content in a carousel. Scroll-*triggered* reveals (play once, 300–500 ms, opacity/transform only) are harmless polish. Scroll-*bound* storytelling has weak or negative evidence: readers read the text and miss the animated graphics ([Kosara](https://eagereyes.org/blog/2016/the-scrollytelling-scourge)). Even CSS-native scroll animations should default to their finished, visible state. They work in Chrome 115+ and Safari 26+, not yet in stable Firefox ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline)), and forcing reduced motion has left content invisible on some sites ([W3C CSSWG](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-css-archive/2022Jun/0886.html)).

The repellents are better documented than the attractors.

**Speed** is the strongest. In Deloitte and Google's 2020 study of 37 brands and 30M+ sessions, a 0.1 s mobile speed gain lifted retail conversions 8.4% and travel conversions 10.1% ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions)). Portent found e-commerce conversion peaks at 1–2 s loads, falling from 3.05% at 1 s to 0.67% at 4 s ([Portent](https://www.portent.com/blog/analytics/research-site-speed-hurting-everyone.htm)). In Google's self-reported case studies, Redbus took CLS from 1.65 to 0 and gained 80–100% in mobile conversion ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/vitals-business-impact)). The "good" thresholds are still **LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1**. A poster-less hero video delays LCP, and WebGL shader compilation hurts INP.

**Scroll-jacking** is next. NN/g's 2023 study found it "leads to more user pain than benefits". Users were disoriented and some thought the site was broken. Horizontal scroll-jacks with text were the worst, and mobile made everything worse ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). Smooth-scroll libraries like Lenis (used by Partizan and Hasselblad) are a mild form of it. Preloaders spend the most fragile seconds on nothing, and auto-rotating carousels are ignored or read as ads ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/auto-forwarding/)).

**Low contrast** is the failure that "elegant" sites cause most often. The WebAIM Million 2026 found low-contrast text on **83.9%** of top home pages ([Priority Pixels summary](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/insights/web-accessibility-is-getting-worse-according-to-the-2026-webaim-million-report/)). Inkblot is already safe here. By my calculation from the tokens, Ink Blue `#2f4a8a` on paper `#faf8f4` is about **8:1**, ink `#1f1c18` on paper about **16:1**, and the faintest text grey `#716a5f` about **5:1**. Grey "aesthetic" body text below `--ink-3` is where the site would start failing.

**Motion and video rules.** Any looping hero video or ambient canvas that runs more than 5 s needs a visible pause control under WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A) ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). `prefers-reduced-motion` should switch to a designed static mode, not just turn things off. About 35% of US adults over 40 have experienced vestibular dysfunction ([VEDA](https://vestibular.org/article/what-is-vestibular/about-vestibular-disorders/)).

**Trust gaps** are the last group. NN/g's trust research puts upfront disclosure of price and policies right after design quality ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). For Inkblot that means stating price or free status, platform and signing status at the button. It also means avoiding modals and cookie banners: Inkblot's no-analytics stance makes a cookieless site natural. Unsigned installers trigger Gatekeeper, and Tauri says notarization is required to distribute on macOS outside the App Store ([Tauri](https://v2.tauri.app/distribute/)).

Put together, these give a rule set every gallery idea has to pass:

- The HTML-only render shows the literal headline, a screenshot, the button and the facts.
- Heavy media (WebGL splash, video) loads after LCP, pauses offscreen and has a poster or static fallback. Spline's failed headless render shows why ([spline.design](https://spline.design)).
- No scroll override, preloader, modal or audio.
- One signature motion moment per screen.
- Fonts are self-hosted, subset WOFF2 with metric-matched fallbacks. Multiple weights can add hundreds of KB ([Web Almanac 2025](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/page-weight)).
- About half of global traffic is mobile (53.4% in July 2026 per [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/platform-market-share/desktop-mobile/worldwide)), so phones get a still image, no WebGL and the "email me the link" swap.

## A recommended synthesis for Inkblot

The galleries and the evidence point to one direction: **"Film house on paper."** Take MUBI's and Metrograph's system (grotesk UI, text-serif display, one deep blue, a cream ground), which is almost exactly Inkblot's tokens. Add **Trim's credit blocks and Tillmans' spec lines in Courier Prime** for every piece of metadata, **NEON's 2.39:1 letterboxed reel** for the hero product loop, and **Jack Davison's Index/Thumbs toggle** for the feature index and timeline. Then let **one** ink moment carry the avant-garde charge. That moment could be the `splash.py`-generated splash blooming once behind the headline, the i-dot of the hero wordmark landing as the page settles, or a Why Zero-style signup that gives each subscriber their own generated splash. Implement it with a light WebGL ink shader or Rive, gated behind reduced-motion and a static SVG fallback. Pendragon and David Whyte show the same restraint applied to worldbuilding and literary content. Zed and Warp show how print vocabulary (crop marks, "Fig." plates, folios) can frame the download and timeline sections without decoration. The Paper & Collage and grain directions are the ones to like but not copy, because the brand forbids paper textures. The dark-purple SaaS look of Obsidian and Reflect, and Heptabase's near-identical warm palette, are what Inkblot most needs to look unlike.

The same direction also corrects some of the research's own suggestions. The notes propose Rorschach symmetry, Fraunces, halftone shaders, a Windows/Linux download matrix and an "open-source" trust badge. The brand and product facts rule each one out or redefine it. The mark is an asymmetric splash. The type system is fixed at three faces. Paper textures are banned (halftone is a judgment call). The app is macOS-only in practice and proprietary. The honest trust strip is "local-first, no account, no tracking, open file formats", not "open source". Only the inkblot-dictation crate is MIT. *(Superseded 22 Oct 2026: the app is open source under Apache 2.0, ships on six platforms, and "open source" is now a headline claim.)*

## Pricing: free app, optional supporter license, paid extras around the marketplace

> **Superseded (22 Oct 2026):** Inkblot is now open source (Apache 2.0) and every feature is free, forever. There is no Founding Writer licence and no Inkblot Plus. See the app's `PROMISE.md` and SITE_PLAN.md → Pricing.

*Added 14 Oct 2026 after the owner chose this approach. It is based on the repo's issues (#23, #33, #34, #37, #57, #65–#70) and on general knowledge of the market. The prices of comparable products quoted below were not re-checked and should be verified before they are quoted publicly.*

**What the marketplace is.** Issue #57 describes a **peer-review exchange**, not a store. Writers post work, other writers review it, and reviewers earn coins. Coins are "earn-only for v1": they are earned by reviewing and spent on posting and spotlight, and they "can't be bought or cashed out" (#68). The marketplace needs accounts (#23, which are optional), Review Mode (#33) and a backend that hasn't been chosen. It is the only part of Inkblot, along with the peer-to-peer relay (#34), that costs money to run for each user. Everything else runs on the writer's Mac.

**Why the marketplace shouldn't be the paywall.** Tying revenue to the part that costs money is right, but charging for access to the exchange would undercut it:
- **Reviewers are the supply.** Every review a writer receives needs another writer to give one. A fee to join means fewer members, slower reviews and worse service for the people who paid.
- **Coins already ration posting.** Charging money on top of that charges people twice.
- **It is the furthest-off item on the roadmap.** It is an XL tracking issue with six parts, depends on two other XL issues, and has no backend yet. If it were the only thing for sale, there would be no revenue for a long time.
- **Precedent:** Scribophile and Critique Circle, the established critique-for-credits communities, keep the exchange free and sell a premium membership for extras.

**Options considered**

| Option | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| A. Free app, paid marketplace | Simple; revenue matches server costs | Shrinks the reviewer pool; no revenue until the marketplace ships |
| **B. Free app, paid marketplace extras** | Exchange stays full; the people who pay are the ones who use server resources | Same wait for revenue; needs extras people value |
| **C. Free app, one-time supporter license now** (Obsidian Catalyst model) | Revenue from the biggest fans today; nothing locked | Small and unpredictable; doesn't cover server costs long term |
| D. Paid publishing tools (typesetting, EPUB, covers checked against KDP and IngramSpark) | This is where Inkblot replaces paid tools like Vellum and Atticus | Breaks "everything else free"; puts a paywall on the biggest differentiator |
| E. Cheap one-time price for the whole app | Simple; needs no servers | Fewer downloads, which starves the marketplace later |
| F. Subscription for everything | Predictable income | Feels expensive; writers resent subscriptions |

**Chosen approach: C now, then B when the marketplace ships.**

1. **Now:** Inkblot is free, with no account and no time limit. An optional one-time **"Founding Writer" license (about $25–40)** gets a profile badge (#23), early beta builds and a thank-you. It never unlocks features.
2. **When the marketplace launches:** reviewing, earning coins and posting stay free. A small **"Inkblot Plus" (about $3–5 a month or $30–40 a year)** adds things that cost money to run or that heavy users want: more active posts at once, private "by request" reviewer circles, larger uploads, the #34 internet relay for co-writing, and extra profile options.
3. **Founding Writers get Plus free** for a set period or for life. That rewards early supporters and helps fill the exchange at launch.

**Rules to hold**
- **Never sell coins or spotlight.** Earning coins by reviewing is what makes the exchange fair. If money buys spotlight (#66) or reviews, it becomes paid reviews by another route, and honest writers leave. Keep coins earn-only after v1 too.
- **Never charge for anything that runs only on the writer's Mac.** This matches the privacy promise in PRIVACY.md and gives the site a clean line: *"Free. Forever, for everything on your Mac."*
- **Plan for the fixed costs.** Apple's Developer Program costs $99 a year and is needed to sign and notarize the app. Add the marketplace backend once one is chosen. Sell licenses through a merchant of record such as Paddle or Lemon Squeezy (roughly 5% + 50¢ per sale) so sales tax and VAT are handled for you.
- **If an iOS version ships,** selling digital extras inside the iOS app generally has to go through Apple's in-app purchase (15% under the Small Business Program). Selling Plus on the web from day one keeps fees lower wherever Apple's rules allow it.

**What changes on the site**
- **Download spec line:** *Free · Version 0.x · [date] · macOS [min] · Apple silicon & Intel · [size]*. Stating "Free" next to the button answers the price question that NN/g's trust research places near the top.
- **Support link:** a quiet "Become a Founding Writer" link in the header or footer and in the closing download block. It must not appear as a modal or interstitial.
- **FAQ entries:** "Is it really free?", "What does a Founding Writer license get me?", and, later, "What is Inkblot Plus?"
- **Timeline:** list the marketplace under Next or Exploring, and mark it "free to join".

## Chosen direction: aspen shadows on paper

*Added 14 Oct 2026, after the owner rated 118 of the 121 gallery sites and answered three rounds of design questions. These decisions replace the earlier "film house on paper" recommendation where the two conflict. Some of them change the app's own brand as well as the site.*

**What the ratings showed.** The owner was very selective: 76 No, 24 Meh, 11 Like and 7 Love.
- **Loves:** MONOLOG, Lifeworld, Pangram Pangram, Stripe Press, Nadav Kander, Smuggler and Tracing Art.
- **Likes:** Harbor, Pulse Films, Trim, Psyop, A24, Iconoclast, The Mill, Tyler Mitchell, Oryzo, Lusion and Floema.
- **Best-scoring direction:** "Ink-on-paper typography", averaging 1.6 out of 3. Every other direction scored 1.1 or lower.
- **Most-tagged reason:** "Type", on 10 of the 18 liked or loved sites.
- **Rejected almost entirely:** literary-serif product sites, paper and collage, generative heroes, photobook publishers and worldbuilding competitors.

The taste is big statement type over quiet photographic imagery, not a "writing app" serif look. The owner also said that leaves blowing in the wind matter to them personally and that they love grainy, textured images.

**Decisions**

| Area | Decision | Changes the app's brand? |
|---|---|---|
| Imagery theme | **Outdoor places that inspire writing.** Leaves are the landing page's place, not the brand itself | No: the places are **website only**; the app stays clean |
| Hero imagery | **Shadows of aspen/birch leaves** falling on the paper ground, like sunlight through a window onto a desk | No (site only) |
| Other places | **Grainy photographs** (stills or short silent loops) of real places, **one per feature**: e.g. open fields, ocean, night sky. See the mapping below | No (site only) |
| Hero content | A **real Inkblot app window lying on the page**, with the shadows passing over it too; headline and "Download for macOS" above it | No |
| Motion | **Barely**: a slow drift you notice after a few seconds; no reaction to the cursor or scrolling | No |
| Grain | **On imagery only** (the shadow layer, photos, video). Text and UI surfaces stay clean | No. DESIGN.md's "no paper textures" rule still holds for UI |
| Headline face | **Inter Display**, set very large and tight, in **sentence case** | Yes: Inter Display becomes the brand display face; Literata stays for reading text and the writer's words |
| Signature colour | **Fern ink `#2f5d46`** replaces Ink Blue `#2f4a8a` | Yes: brand colour and default accent |
| Logo | Keep the "ib" splash monogram; **it may now appear in Fern ink** as well as black | Yes: relaxes "never takes Ink Blue" to allow the new brand green |
| Dark mode | **Moonlight**: dark ground with pale, cool light patches through the leaves | Site follows the system theme |

**Suggested place for each feature** (a first proposal; the owner may swap any of them)

| Feature section | Place | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Landing hero | Aspen leaf shadows on paper | The calm moment before writing |
| Drafting (manuscript editor, focus) | A flat open field under a wide sky | Empty space, nothing in the way |
| Worldbuilding maps | Coastline from a cliff, or open ocean | Coasts and seas are what maps are made of |
| Planet and galaxy maps | Night sky over a field | The 3D, planet and galaxy map formats |
| Screenplays | An empty road or pier at dusk | Cinematic and wide; suits 2.39:1 framing |
| Story timeline and continuity | Tide line on a beach | Time passing, marks left and washed away |
| Dictation and read-aloud | Forest in rain | Sound and voice; rain is the most-recorded "ambient" sound |
| Typesetting and publishing | A quiet harbour at dawn | Finished work setting out |
| Privacy and local-first | A remote cabin or hillside | Your work stays with you, offline |

**How the photographs should look, so the places read as one set**
- **One grade for all:** desaturated and slightly cool, with lifted blacks, so a field and an ocean look like they belong to the same roll of film. Fern ink appears only in the type, never as a colour tint on the photos.
- **Grain on every image,** matching the grain on the hero's leaf shadows. This is the visual thread that links the abstract hero to the photographic sections.
- **A caption on each image in Courier Prime,** like a photographer's or a writer's note, e.g. *"Fig. 3 · Open field, Kansas · 7:12 pm"*. This fits the owner's Likes for film-credit and metadata styling (Trim, Pulse, Harbor).
- **Motion stays as quiet as the hero.** Prefer stills, or silent loops of 6–10 seconds that start only when visible, use a poster frame, and pause when the visitor asks for reduced motion.
- **Sourcing:** shoot your own (the most personal and the most distinctive), or license from photographers. Avoid recognizable free stock: Unsplash's most popular fields and seas appear on thousands of sites.

**Notes for building it**
- **Contrast.** Fern ink on paper `#faf8f4` works out to about 7:1 (my calculation), which passes WCAG AA and AAA for body text. Dark mode needs a lighter green. The app's existing dark `--ink-green: #86c79a` is a good start, but check it against the final dark ground.
- **Making the shadows.** The cheapest high-quality route is to film real aspen shadows on a sunny day against a white wall or sheet. In DaVinci Resolve, make the footage monochrome, soften it, add grain, and export a 10–15 second seamless loop (1080p, H.264/HEVC, aim for under 3 MB). Lay it over the page with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`; dark mode uses an inverted version with `screen`. A procedural shader is the alternative: it's tiny and endlessly varied, but harder to make look real.
- **Keeping it accessible.** Use a still poster frame when the visitor asks for reduced motion. WCAG 2.2.2 requires a way to pause motion that lasts more than 5 seconds, so add a small, quiet pause control. Keep shadows light where the headline and button sit.
- **What it replaces.** The earlier advice ("one ink moment", no grain, Literata as display face, Ink Blue) is superseded where it conflicts with this table. Restraint, the Courier Prime spec line, the section order and the timeline advice all still apply.

## Conclusion

The owner's instinct toward film and photography sites is better than a hunch: it describes Inkblot's own brand. The admired film houses and photo publishers already run on warm paper, sepia-black ink, one deep accent, a text serif and typewritten metadata, and those are Inkblot's tokens. The work is curation and restraint, not invention. The one thing those sites lack is what Inkblot uniquely has: a physically modelled ink splash and a product that turns words into typeset books and drawn worlds. That is where the avant-garde budget should go, and only there.

The research also changes what the "timeline of upcoming features" should be. The evidence gives no reason to expect a roadmap to drive downloads, and good reason to expect stale promises to hurt. Inkblot's eight months of dense, dated shipping history is its strongest proof of life. Presented as film-style credit blocks with a short, confidence-filtered "Now / Next" on top, the timeline becomes the site's best trust signal rather than a list of promises.
