Chapter three

The Lamp Room

The stairs turned seven times before the lamp room, and Ada counted every one, because counting kept the dark at the window where it belonged. Above her the lens waited, cold and patient, a cathedral of glass no wider than a kitchen table.

She had promised her father she would light it at dusk. It was well past dusk. The sea had gone the colour of slate, then of nothing at all.

The marketplace

Read each other.

Real human readers. Post a chapter, a poem, a story or a script, and other writers review it, line by line.

  • Coins can't be bought. Every review is earned.
  • Take on a work and you finish it.
  • Writers rate every review.
  • Report a reviewer who isn't human.

The world is yours. So is every word.

  • No AI writes for you.

    Nothing in Inkblot writes, rewrites or summarises for you.

  • Never trained on your work.

    We'll never use your writing to train AI, or sell or license it to anyone who would.

  • Marked to every reader.

    Each shared copy carries a hidden watermark that traces back to whoever read it.

  • Hard to copy.

    Screenshots and copying are blocked in the review view, wherever the device allows.

  • Leaks get traced.

    Find your work online? Report it, and the mark names who shared it.

  • Open to check.

    Every line of Inkblot is public. Don't take these promises on trust; read them in the code.

Every shared copy carries a mark like the one under your cursor. You'd never see it in the text.

Free.

Every feature, on every device, for everyone. Forever.

Built for modest, older and shared devices too, because those are the devices most people have.
