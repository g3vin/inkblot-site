# C · The atlas

**Concept.** Worldbuilding first. "Write." sits on a drawn graticule with a dashed route
running off the edge. The first product shown is a map, not a page. The journey is drawn
as a route with six stops, the world tools sit in a bento of app windows (the map
largest), and then "it becomes a book". This is the direction no competitor occupies:
the worldbuilding tools have no typesetting, and the formatting tools have no world.

**Target emotion.** Possibility: a world big enough to get lost in, with a way home to a
finished book.

**Sub-line tested:** "Draft it, map it, typeset it. One free app from first line to
finished book, and nothing in it writes for you." (audit option 2).

**Type.**
- Inter Display Bold for headlines (−6.5% on "Write.").
- Inter for the tool descriptions, which are shorter and UI-like.
- Literata for longer reading text.
- Courier Prime for stop numbers and spec lines.
- The graticule and route are inline SVG: lines in the theme's rule colour, the route in
  Fern. They're drawn, not imagery.

**Palette and contrast.** The same tokens: ink on paper 16.0, muted 5.5, Fern 9.4, white
on Fern 10.0; Ink theme 15.9 / 7.0 / Fern 8.2. The graticule uses `--line-strong` and is
decorative (`aria-hidden`). The route's Fern meets 3:1 non-text contrast in every theme
(paper 9.4, Ink 8.2, Black 7.9).

**Trade-offs.**
- It's the strongest differentiator, and it speaks to a passionate niche (worldbuilders,
  fantasy and SF writers).
- It needs the best screenshots of the four: a beautiful map is the hero, and an empty
  or ugly one would sink it.
- "Draft it, map it, typeset it" is the most informative sub-line, and also the longest.
- Literary-fiction writers may feel it isn't for them.

**Who it would appeal to most:** worldbuilders, fantasy and SF novelists, game writers,
and Campfire and World Anvil users.
**Who it might lose:** memoirists, poets and literary novelists who never draw a map.
